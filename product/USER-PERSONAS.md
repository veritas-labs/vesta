# Vesta — User Personas

Three primary personas. One secondary. Each represents a distinct household shape.

---

## 1. Sarah — The Busy Parent

**Profile:** 38, married, two kids (8 and 12). Works full-time in healthcare. Household runs on mental lists, a shared Google Calendar that's 60% accurate, and a WhatsApp group that's mostly memes.

**Household shape:** Traditional family, but both parents work. Kids are old enough to participate but need structure.

**Key needs:**
- Divide and track household responsibilities without a "nagging" dynamic
- Keep kids accountable for their chores without it becoming a battle
- Coordinate schedules with minimal friction ("who's doing school pickup Thursday?")

**Pain points:**
- Current tools assume one person manages everything
- Task delegation feels like one more thing to manage, not less
- Information lives in too many places (calendar, notes, messages, memory)

**Vesta value proposition:** "A single place to see what needs doing, who's doing it, and whether it got done — without becoming household project manager as a second job."

---

## 2. Arthur — The Independent Retiree

**Profile:** 74, lives alone since his wife passed. Semi-retired, still sharp. Has a home helper who comes twice a week. Family checks in regularly but doesn't live nearby.

**Household shape:** Single-person household with occasional support.

**Key needs:**
- Simple reminders for recurring tasks (medication, bin collection, bill payments)
- Easy way to communicate needs to his home helper ("could you pick up laundry detergent?")
- Family can see a high-level view if he chooses to share it (not surveillance — transparency)

**Pain points:**
- Most apps are too complex, too noisy, or assume tech-savviness he doesn't have
- Existing reminder apps are dumb — same time every day regardless of context
- Feels "managed" by family rather than in control of his own home

**Vesta value proposition:** "Simple tools that help you stay independent, with the option to share what you want, with who you want, when you want."

---

## 3. Priya, Jake, and Tom — The Flatmates

**Profile:** Three professionals sharing a 2-bed flat in a city. Ages 26-30. Joined by convenience, not deep friendship. Want to be good flatmates but coordination is annoying.

**Household shape:** Unrelated adults, financial co-dependence, social distance.

**Key needs:**
- Fair chore rotation that nobody has to police manually
- Shared expenses (rent, utilities, groceries) tracked transparently
- Clear rules for shared spaces (dishes, bin rota, guests) without awkward conversations

**Pain points:**
- The person who cares most ends up doing everything
- Money conversations are awkward and get avoided
- No shared view of household state — everyone assumes someone else did the bins

**Vesta value proposition:** "Shared accountability without the awkwardness. If the system says it's your turn for the bins, it's your turn for the bins."

---

## 4. Mei — The Household Coordinator (Secondary)

**Profile:** 45, manages a multi-generational household (her mother, her two kids, her partner). Works part-time. Essentially the "household CEO" who resents the role.

**Household shape:** Extended family, complex dynamics, cultural expectations.

**Key needs:**
- Delegate tasks to household members who aren't fully onboard with "the system"
- Visibility into what's happening across the household without micromanaging
- Handle varying levels of tech comfort (her mum won't use a complex app)

**Pain points:**
- Every tool she's tried requires too much setup and maintenance
- Her mum finds apps confusing; her partner finds them unnecessary
- She ends up being the system, not using the system

**Vesta value proposition:** "Set it up once, let it run. The household coordinates through the tool, not through you."

---

## Key Takeaway Across All Personas

The common thread: **household management should not fall on one person's shoulders, and the tools should reduce mental load, not add to it.** Every feature we build should pass the test: "Does this shift work from the coordinator to the system?"

---

## Accessibility & Age Range

- **Age range: 8–100.** Vesta spans children to the very elderly.
- **Accessibility is non-negotiable.** Software must be simple and clear enough for a grandma with poor visibility to use comfortably. This is a product requirement, not a nice-to-have.
- **Themes.** The app supports themes to adapt to the user — e.g., a kid-friendly mode when a child is using it. Theming serves both accessibility needs and age-appropriateness.
